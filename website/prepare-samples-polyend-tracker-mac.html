Preparing Samples for Polyend Tracker+ and Tracker

Polyend makes three Trackers, and they treat stereo differently. Tracker+ and Tracker Mini play stereo samples in stereo. The first Tracker plays mono only. On all three, a project's sample memory is limited, and a mono sample takes half the memory of the same sound in stereo. Here is what each model expects, and when converting to mono on your Mac is worth it.

What Each Tracker Expects

All three read uncompressed WAV files from the microSD card, and Polyend's manuals name 44.1 kHz, 16-bit as the format to load. The sampler engine also reads 24-bit and 32-bit float WAV.

  • Tracker+: stereo or mono. A project holds about 12 minutes of mono samples, or about 6 minutes of stereo.
  • Tracker Mini: stereo or mono, with the same project memory as Tracker+.
  • Tracker (first model): mono only. Imported audio is converted to 44.1 kHz, 16-bit mono, and a project holds about 133 seconds of samples.

When Mono Helps

On Tracker+ and Tracker Mini, stereo is a choice, and it has a price: a stereo sample uses twice the project memory of the same sound in mono. Drums, one-shots and bass rarely need width, so converting those to mono doubles the sample time you have left for the rest. Keep stereo for pads, textures and loops where the width is the point.

On the first Tracker, every sample ends up mono anyway, and its project memory is small. Converting on your Mac first lets you hear the mono result before it goes on the card, and halves the size of every file.

Either way, a proper mono conversion sums the left and right channels, so a sound panned to one side isn't lost.

How to Batch Convert for Polyend Tracker

Batch convert the samples that don't need stereo in one go:

  1. Download It's mono, yo! from the Mac App Store
  2. Drag in the samples you want in mono, or a whole folder of them. On Tracker+ and Tracker Mini, leave out the ones you want to keep in stereo.
  3. Set Output Settings to WAV / 16-bit / 44.1 kHz
  4. Pick an output folder (e.g. "Tracker Mono Samples")
  5. Press Start, then copy the folder into the Samples folder on the microSD card

To reach the card, put it in your Mac with a card reader. Tracker and Tracker+ come with an adapter for this. Tracker+ and Tracker Mini can also show the card to your Mac over USB: turn on USB Storage in the Config menu.

Polyend Tracker Prep Tips

  • Sort before you convert: put drums and one-shots in one folder and pads and textures in another, then convert only the first.
  • Watch the memory bar: the sample browser shows how much project memory a sample would take before you import it.
  • Trim silence: every second of a sample counts against the project's sample time.
  • Mono before Low Quality: Tracker+ and Tracker Mini can also import at a lower quality to save memory. Mono saves the same kind of room without lowering the sound quality of a sample that doesn't need width.
  • 16-bit is enough: Tracker+ stores samples in its instruments as 44.1 kHz, 16-bit.
